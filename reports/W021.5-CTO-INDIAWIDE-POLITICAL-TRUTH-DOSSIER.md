## 1. Executive Summary & Root-Cause Findings

### 1.1 Objective & Executive Mandate
In accordance with Directive `W021.5-CTO-INDIAWIDE-POLITICAL-TRUTH-AUDIT-AND-REMEDIATION-014`, an exhaustive, repository-wide investigation into political ground truth, temporal consistency, source provenance, and cross-layer data propagation was conducted across all 31 Indian state and union territory legislative assemblies.

The investigation was anchored by the confirmed defect in **AC 61 Gangavathi (Koppal district, Karnataka)**, where the 2023 General Election winner was erroneously recorded as `Paratap Gouda Patil (INC)` instead of **`G. Janardhan Reddy (KRPP)`**. 

The scope was not limited to Karnataka. An automated static analysis of all 204 files in `data/seed/` and cross-layer consumers in `apps/api` and `apps/mobile` was executed to determine whether the Gangavathi defect was an isolated typo or a symptom of systemic data extraction, normalization, and temporal modeling vulnerabilities.

### 1.2 Core Root-Cause Analysis
The audit uncovered four systemic failure modes:
1. **Unvalidated Web/Wikipedia Scraping:** Seed data was populated by merging disparate web scrapings (Wikipedia, MyNeta, local election tables) without automated cross-validation against official Election Commission of India (ECI) Form 20 (Final Result Sheet) or Form 21E (Return of Election).
2. **Synthetic / Transposed Placeholder Propagation:** In the Karnataka dataset, 11 constituencies contained synthetic names suffixed with `" Jr"` (e.g., `R. V. Devaraj Jr`), and ~100 constituencies retained stale 2018 winners as 2023 results. In Gangavathi, a 2018 typo (`Paratap Gouda` at AC 76) was accidentally expanded and transposed to AC 61 Gangavathi for 2023.
3. **Absence of Temporal Validity Modeling (General vs. By-Election):** The repository models sitting representatives as scalar string attributes (`winner2024 ?? winner2023 ?? ...`) on static constituency records. When an elected member resigns, passes away, or is disqualified (e.g., Pushkar Singh Dhami winning the Champawat by-election in Uttarakhand; Madan Bhaiya winning Khatauli in UP), the constituency seed retains the General Election winner while the MLA profile reflects the by-election winner, creating an unreconciled cross-file discrepancy.
4. **Premature Simulation / Projection Coupling:** In Tamil Nadu and West Bengal, future 2026 speculative projections (`winner2026: 'TVK'`, 99 seats in TN) were introduced directly into constituency records and consumed by client adapters (`stateDataAdapter.ts`) as current reality.

## 6. Source-Provenance & Temporal-Integrity Findings

### 6.1 Fragility in Sitting MLA Resolution
In `apps/api/src/services/stateData.ts` (lines 55–56) and `apps/mobile/lib/stateDataAdapter.ts` (lines 16–30), the sitting MLA is determined via a fallback chain:
```typescript
const winner = c.winner2026 ?? c.winner2024 ?? c.winner2023 ?? c.winner2022 ?? c.winner2021 ?? ...
```
**Architectural Hazards Identified:**
1. **Blind to Vacancies & By-Elections:** A general election winner remains bound even after death, resignation, or by-election defeat.
2. **Pollution by Speculative Projections:** If a file introduces a `winner2026` field (as done in TN for actor Vijay's TVK party), the fallback chain immediately presents the speculative 2026 projection as the current reality in the mobile interface.
3. **Party Defections Ignored:** `currentParty` is often not synchronized when MLAs switch parties post-election.
