## Exact original 2008 schedule facts

The original PDF's own Table of Contents identifies:
- Schedule I: allocation of Lok Sabha seats.
- Schedule II: number of seats in various Legislative Assemblies.
- Schedule III: Andhra Pradesh (composite State), pages 8–32.
- Original Schedule XXXI: Delhi, pages 543–556.
- Original Schedule XXXII: Puducherry, pages 557–560.
- Jammu and Kashmir Assembly constituencies are in an Annexure.

For original Schedule III (composite Andhra Pradesh), Table A contains Assembly Constituency territorial extents; Table B contains Parliamentary Constituency extents. Do not label the AP Assembly extents as Schedule II or label Telangana as original Schedule XXXI.

## Successor-state legal amendment

A government-hosted copy of the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014 was acquired and hash-verified:
- URL: https://apseeds.ap.gov.in/PDF/apreorg/APRegACT2014.pdf
- Size: 231,596 bytes; SHA-256: `84cd566b917f9a3f600c47f6540520c950166b424a964ad37d64dc3c968114aa`
- 71 pages; byte identity against the India Code-hosted copy remains unverified.

The Act's Section 15 and Second Schedule amend the 2008 Order. They replace Schedule III with “Schedule III — Andhra Pradesh — Table A: Assembly Constituencies” comprising the 175 successor-State AP extents, and insert after Schedule XXVI “Schedule XXVII — Telangana — Table A: Assembly Constituencies” with the 119 Telangana extents. This Act does not justify the prior dossier's claim that Schedule XXXI is Telangana. For states after the inserted schedule, the exact numbering in a later consolidated text must be verified from that text, not guessed from a base-order file.

## Seat totals and source boundaries

The original 2008 Order covers the historical composite State of Andhra Pradesh (294 ACs and 42 PCs). The 2014 Act reallocates the current seat lists to Andhra Pradesh (175 ACs; 29 SC and 7 ST) and Telangana (119 ACs; 19 SC and 12 ST). The 2008 Order alone is not a post-bifurcation legal text; use the 2014 Act's amendment as an overlay, and retain the 2015 Removal of Difficulties Order and later notified corrections as separate legal instruments.
